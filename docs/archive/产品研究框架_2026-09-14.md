## 1. Project Context

We are helping a real-estate flipping company define and build a new AI-powered operating platform for house flipping.

The client does **not yet have a fully defined product requirement**. Current needs are fragmented, informal, and sometimes expressed as examples rather than specifications. Our responsibility is therefore not only software implementation, but also:

- Product discovery
- Competitive research
- Workflow decomposition
- Data architecture thinking
- AI system design
- MVP definition
- Validation of business value before overbuilding

Do **not** assume the client already knows exactly what should be built.

Our job is to convert vague business pain into a clear product architecture and prioritized MVP.

---

## 2. Core Product Thesis

Do not think of this as simply a "flip house app."

The larger product should be understood as a potential:

# Flip House Operating System

with three major layers:

### Layer 1 — Data / Operational System
Internal source of truth for property, project, budget, document, renovation, contractor, and transaction data.

### Layer 2 — Intelligence / Decision System
AI-assisted underwriting, market analysis, renovation strategy, design recommendations, budget estimation, resale reasoning, and decision support.

### Layer 3 — Execution / Field System
Project tracking, contractor coordination, construction progress, photo updates, tasks, change orders, budget actuals, and field reporting.

These layers should be treated separately during discovery, even if they later converge into one product.

---

## 3. Current Priority: Product & AI Intelligence Research

Our immediate research focus is the second major workstream:

# Flip House Product & AI Intelligence Landscape

The purpose of this research is to answer:

> What has already been solved by existing real-estate / flipping / construction products, what should we integrate instead of rebuild, and where can our AI product create differentiated value?

This report is not a generic competitor list.

It must help us decide:

- What capabilities are table stakes
- What capabilities are already commoditized
- What capabilities should be integrated through external APIs
- What capabilities are worth building internally
- What capabilities can become our AI differentiation
- What should be included in the first MVP
- What should explicitly be postponed

## 5. Core AI Product Concept

The client gave an example similar to:

> "I enter a property address, and the system should tell me whether the price makes sense, what nearby successful homes look like, what architectural / renovation style will likely maximize resale value, what renovation strategy to use, what it may cost, and why."

This should **not** be implemented as an LLM guessing from general knowledge.

The correct mental model is:

```text
                        USER
                         │
                  Property Address
                         │
                         ▼
                  AI ORCHESTRATOR
                         │
        ┌────────────────┼────────────────┐
        ▼                ▼                ▼
 Property / Public   Market / Comps   Internal Company Data
      Data               Data              Data
        │                │                 │
        ▼                ▼                 ▼
 Parcel / Tax        Comparable Sales   Historical Flips
 MLS / Listing       ARV / AVM          Rehab Cost History
 Permit / Zoning     Price per Sq Ft    Contractor Performance
 Neighborhood        Market Trend       Actual vs Estimated
 Images              DOM / Liquidity    Realized ROI
        │                │                 │
        └────────────────┼─────────────────┘
                         ▼
                   DECISION ENGINE
                         │
          ┌──────────────┼───────────────┐
          ▼              ▼               ▼
        BUY?          DESIGN          BUDGET
          │              │               │
          └──────────────┼───────────────┘
                         ▼
                    AI RESPONSE
```

The LLM should primarily:

- Understand user intent
- Call tools / APIs
- Retrieve structured evidence
- Combine market and internal data
- Explain recommendations
- Generate an auditable reasoning summary

The LLM should **not** invent:

- Property values
- ARV
- comps
- renovation costs
- neighborhood trends
- ROI
- construction status

when authoritative data sources are available or required.

## 6. Important Product Question

One of the most valuable product questions is not:

> "Can AI redesign this house?"

It is:

> "What renovation strategy is most likely to maximize resale value for this specific property, in this specific neighborhood, at this specific acquisition price and budget?"

This distinction matters.

The system should ultimately connect:

- Property characteristics
- Local buyer preference
- Comparable renovated homes
- Architecture/style patterns
- Price per square foot
- Renovation cost
- Expected ARV
- Estimated margin
- Time to sale
- Risk

This turns design generation into an investment decision tool rather than a visual gimmick.

## 10. Strategic Principle: Build vs Buy vs Integrate

For every major capability, classify it as:

### BUILD
Build internally when it represents:
- Company-specific workflow
- Proprietary historical learning
- Unique AI orchestration
- Decision logic
- Cross-source synthesis
- UX differentiation

### BUY / API
Prefer external data or services for:
- Parcel records
- Tax records
- Property metadata
- Comps
- AVM
- MLS-derived data where licensed
- Maps / geocoding
- Market data

### INTEGRATE
Integrate existing tools when replacement creates little strategic advantage.

Do not recreate mature commodity infrastructure purely because we can code it.

## 11. Our Potential Differentiation

Do not assume the differentiation is simply "we have AI."

Likely higher-value differentiation areas include:

### 1. Company-Specific Historical Learning
Use the company's completed flips to learn:

- Estimated vs actual renovation cost
- Category-level overruns
- Contractor performance
- Renovation choices
- Time to completion
- ARV prediction error
- Realized resale value
- Neighborhood-specific results

### 2. Decision Intelligence
Combine external property data with internal history to answer:

- Should we buy this property?
- At what price?
- What is the likely ARV?
- What renovation level is justified?
- What should we spend money on?
- What should we avoid spending money on?

### 3. Renovation Strategy Intelligence
Recommend design direction based on:

- Relevant successful comps
- Local architectural patterns
- Target buyer profile
- Current property structure
- Budget constraints
- Resale impact

### 4. Evidence-Based AI
Every recommendation should preferably show:

- supporting comps
- internal historical examples
- assumptions
- confidence
- sensitivity

rather than presenting unsupported AI answers.

### 5. Closed Learning Loop
The system should eventually learn from:

```text
Estimate → Decision → Renovation → Actual Cost → Sale → Outcome → Better Next Estimate
```

This feedback loop is potentially much more defensible than a generic real-estate chatbot.

## 12. MVP Philosophy

Do not begin by trying to define or build the complete platform.

Use:

# Discovery → Product Hypothesis → MVP Probe → Validation → Expansion

The MVP should validate the **highest-value decision**, not merely demonstrate that we can build a UI.

### Recommended Sequence

#### MVP 0 — Data Visibility
Build one excellent "Property 360" view for a real completed or active company property.

Goal:
- Prove we can unify fragmented information into a useful decision view.

#### MVP 1 — AI Property Brief
Input:

```text
Property address
```

Output:

```text
Property
→ Market
→ Comps
→ ARV
→ Recommended purchase range
→ Renovation strategy
→ Style recommendation
→ Rehab budget
→ Expected ROI
→ Risks
```

Goal:
- Validate whether management finds AI-assisted acquisition and renovation decisions materially useful.

#### MVP 2 — Project / Renovation Dashboard
Add:
- Budget vs actual
- Timeline
- milestones
- project health
- cost variance

#### MVP 3 — Contractor / Field Workflow
Add only after workflow is understood:
- Daily logs
- progress photos
- tasks
- issue reporting
- change orders
- contractor updates

Do not prematurely build the entire field app before we know how the company actually operates.

## 14. Research Quality Standards

When researching competitors or APIs:

1. Prefer first-party product documentation.
2. Verify capabilities instead of inferring from marketing language.
3. Record pricing when available.
4. Record API availability when relevant.
5. Record data coverage / geographic limitations.
6. Record whether features are desktop, mobile, or both.
7. Record whether the feature is actually AI or simple automation.
8. Distinguish current product capability from roadmap / marketing claims.
9. Capture screenshots or source links when useful for internal review.
10. Note uncertainty explicitly.

Do not present unverified claims as facts.

---

## 15. AI Reliability Rules

For high-impact recommendations such as purchase price, ARV, budget, or ROI:

The product should favor:

```text
Structured Data
+ Deterministic Calculations
+ Retrieval / APIs
+ Historical Company Data
+ LLM Explanation
```

over:

```text
LLM Guessing
```

Where possible, outputs should include:

- data source
- date
- assumption
- confidence
- calculation
- supporting evidence

The AI assistant should be explainable enough that an experienced real-estate operator can challenge or override it.

## 16. Important Non-Goals for Early Phase

Avoid prematurely committing to:

- A complete ERP
- A replacement for every Lark workflow
- A complete contractor app
- Full construction accounting
- Custom AVM from scratch
- Custom MLS infrastructure
- A massive consumer-facing app
- AI image generation as the core product
- A chatbot without reliable data tools

These may be explored later, but should not dominate initial MVP development.

## 17. How Claude Should Help on This Project

When asked to research, design, or implement for this project, Claude should:

1. Start from the house-flipping lifecycle.
2. Separate data, intelligence, and execution problems.
3. Identify the actual business decision behind a requested feature.
4. Avoid treating vague client examples as final requirements.
5. Compare against existing products before proposing custom development.
6. Prefer integration for commodity capabilities.
7. Focus custom development on company-specific intelligence and workflow.
8. Design AI as an orchestrator over trusted tools and data.
9. Require evidence for financial / valuation recommendations.
10. Optimize MVPs for learning and business validation.
11. Preserve a path toward a larger Flip House Operating System without overbuilding early.

## 18. Default Product Lens

Whenever a new feature is proposed, answer these questions:

```text
1. Who is the user?
2. What decision are they trying to make?
3. At what stage of the flip lifecycle?
4. What data is required?
5. Where does that data come from?
6. Is this already solved by another product/API?
7. Should we build, buy, or integrate?
8. What is the smallest useful version?
9. How do we measure whether it created value?
10. How does the outcome feed back into future decisions?
```

If these questions cannot yet be answered, treat the feature as a discovery item rather than a committed requirement.

## 19. Working Product Vision

A strong long-term vision is:

> A company-specific AI operating system for house flipping that turns property data, market intelligence, historical company performance, renovation strategy, project execution, and realized sale outcomes into one continuous decision-and-learning loop.

But this is a **working product thesis**, not a locked specification.

We should continuously refine it as we inspect real company data, workflows, completed flips, and user behavior.
